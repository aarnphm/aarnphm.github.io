Mary knows every physical fact about color vision before she ever sees color. When she sees red, Jackson's original claim is that she learns a new fact, so complete physical knowledge did not give her all the facts. [@jackson1982epiphenomenalqualia; @jackson1986whatmary]

the reply menu:

- ability reply: Mary gains a new ability, such as recognizing and imagining red, rather than a new fact. [@lewis1988know; @nemirow1990physicalism]
- acquaintance reply: Mary gains direct acquaintance with red experience, rather than a new public fact.
- phenomenal concept reply: Mary gains a first-person concept for a fact she already knew physically. [@loar1990phenomenal]
- Jackson later backs away from the anti-physicalist conclusion. [@jackson1998argument]

what this note tests is completeness. If a finished physical theory leaves Mary ignorant of what red is like, [[thoughts/physicalism]] owes a story that explains the ignorance without adding a new nonphysical fact.
