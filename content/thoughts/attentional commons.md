Attention has environmental conditions. Reading, thinking, or listening to someone becomes harder when the surrounding space keeps making competing demands.

Matthew Crawford's **attentional commons**: the shared condition of being able to occupy a space without being continually addressed by advertising. Each intrusion can seem too small to contest. Their accumulation changes what the space permits.

Crawford's airport example makes the distribution visible. Advertising occupies the terminal, while the business-class lounge offers relief from it. Access to an environment that supports thought becomes tied to purchasing power. He develops the argument in [“The Cost of Paying Attention”](https://www.speakers.ca/2015/04/the-cost-of-paying-attention/), adapted from _The World Beyond Your Head_ (2015).

The resource being depleted is our freedom to decide what deserves attention. A space can be acoustically quiet and still demand that we read screens, dismiss prompts, and process sales pitches. Crawford's sense of silence includes freedom from those demands.

protecting attention requires decisions about the spaces we share. Who can install a screen, make it speak, or require everyone passing through to encounter a message? The revenue goes to identifiable parties; the interruptions are borne by everyone present.

This connects to [[thoughts/love#attention and revision|attention as a practice of care]]. Sustained attention needs time and conditions that allow us to stay with a person or a thought. Preserving those conditions is something we can do for one another.
