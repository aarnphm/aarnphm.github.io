Reductionism concerns relations between entities, explanations, or theories at different levels. A connection with [[thoughts/Compression|compression]]: explaining several patterns through common underlying processes. The link to [[thoughts/being|becoming]] remains an open question.

1. Ontology: higher-level entities consist of lower-level entities. A biological example is the claim that organisms consist entirely of physical components and their interactions. This makes no claim about a minimum number of parts.
2. Methodology: investigate a system by studying its components and their interactions. Whether this strategy answers a particular question has to be established in that context.
3. Theory: recover one theory's claims from another. In Nagel's account, this involves derivation, sometimes using bridge principles connecting their terms. Corrections and approximations can be part of the relation; the theories' ages do not define it.

These claims can come apart: accepting physical composition alone does not establish that every biological explanation can be reduced to physics. See [reductionism in biology, §1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reduction-biology/) and [scientific reduction, §2](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-reduction/).
