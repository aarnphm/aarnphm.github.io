phenomenal consciousness is the part picked out by Nagel's question, whether there is something it is like for the subject to have the state. [@nagel1974bat] This is why the note lives next to [[thoughts/qualia]] and [[thoughts/access consciousness]].

Block's distinction does the sorting work. Access consciousness is availability for reasoning, report, and action. Phenomenal consciousness is the experiential character of the state. [@block1995confusion] A system can give strong access evidence while the phenomenal question stays open.

Chalmers calls discrimination, integration, report, attention, and control the easy problems because they are functional questions. [@chalmers1995facing] The hard problem asks why those functions come with experience at all. Levine names the same residue the explanatory gap. [@levine1983materialism]

the test i want to keep:

- if an explanation only predicts report, memory, attention, or action, it is an access explanation.
- if it explains why seeing red feels this way from the inside, it is aimed at the phenomenal part.
- most [[thoughts/mechanistic interpretability|interpretability]] work is evidence of the first kind. It can show which internal state supports a report or decision. It needs another premise before it reaches phenomenal consciousness.
