## voluntarism

A doxastic attitude is an attitude toward a proposition. The coarse cases are belief, disbelief, and suspension of judgment. For proposition $p$, I can believe $p$, disbelieve $p$, or suspend judgment about $p$.

Doxastic voluntarism asks whether these attitudes are under the will. I can choose to investigate evidence, avoid a source, or rehearse an argument. Directly choosing belief is harder. I cannot believe $p$ in the same way I can choose to raise my hand.

Responsibility shifts upstream. I may not be able to choose belief directly, but I can choose what evidence I seek and when I stop checking.

re [doxastic voluntarism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/doxastic-voluntarism/)
