A true inventory of facts about a person can still support the wrong interpretation. The facts become understanding only when the person's reasons and distinctions can correct the model built from them.

## the circle

The hermeneutic circle names a movement between part and whole. A sentence changes how the conversation reads. The conversation changes how the sentence reads. Each pass begins from a partial expectation and can revise it [@gadamer1960truth].

There is no view from nowhere at the start. An interpreter brings a language, history, and set of live questions to the encounter. Gadamer calls this fore-understanding. The relevant discipline is to expose those starting points to correction from the text, person, or practice being interpreted.

## horizon and application

A horizon is the range of things that can show up as intelligible from a standpoint. Understanding expands that range. Gadamer's fusion of horizons is this expansion through encounter. It does not require two sealed inner worlds to merge into one.

Application belongs inside understanding. A legal rule, a promise, or a confession has meaning in a case. Gadamer takes Aristotle's _phronesis_ as a model for this situated judgment: inherited concepts guide the interpreter, and the particular case can force those concepts to change [@gadamer1960truth].

## identity and recognition

Ricoeur uses narrative identity to answer the question of who acts. A life remains identifiable through change because a person can relate events, commitments, and revisions as a history [@ricoeur1992oneself]. The narrative can be contested and revised. It is not a fixed essence or a license for another person to author the story alone.

Taylor's account of strong evaluation adds the frameworks within which desires and actions carry weight [@taylor1989sources]. To understand a choice, an interpreter may need to learn which distinctions make one option admirable, shameful, sacred, or trivial for the person choosing.

Honneth gives recognition a social role. Relations of recognition support self-trust, self-respect, and self-esteem, while misrecognition can damage a person's practical relation to themself [@honneth1995struggle]. This claim concerns the conditions of agency and self-relation. Accepting every self-interpretation is a different claim.

## epistemic injustice

Fricker calls a failure hermeneutical injustice when a gap in shared interpretive resources leaves someone unfairly disadvantaged in making sense of a social experience [@fricker2007epistemic]. The problem can occur before any listener judges a report. The available concepts themselves can be too poor or too unevenly shaped to express what happened.

A listener who discounts testimony because of prejudice presents a different failure, which Fricker calls testimonial injustice. Keeping the two mechanisms separate shows where repair has to occur. Testimonial injustice asks for better credibility judgment. Hermeneutical injustice often needs better shared concepts and access to the practices that define them.

## public criteria

[[thoughts/Wittgenstein|Wittgenstein]] gives understanding public criteria in language use [@wittgenstein1953philosophical]. An interpretation earns its place when it supports the right continuation, correction, and participation in a shared practice. Private conviction supplies no exemption from those checks.

For a person, the checks include asking a question that reaches the live distinction, changing action after a correction, and preserving commitments across a new case. These checks expose interpretations that never update.

## connection to [[thoughts/functionalism|functionalism]]

A functional account can test whether the interpreter changes after surprise and correction. These tests are useful because they can fail in public [@levin2024functionalism].

Those tests do not say what the interpreter is optimizing for. The same prediction can serve care or control. Hermeneutics asks whether the interpreter treats the other person as a source of reasons whose account can change the terms of interpretation.

## love and revision

[[/posts/love|Love]] needs a working model of the person because care has to anticipate needs and consequences. The model becomes dangerous when it is defended against the person it describes. The practical bridge is [[thoughts/love#attention and revision|attention and revision]]: attend closely enough to notice the mismatch, then let the person correct the model.
