A digital garden is a collection of linked notes that stay open to revision. A note can begin with a question or an excerpt. Later, it can acquire an explanation, a worked example, or a correction. Keeping these changes on the same page gives the idea an address that other notes can refer to. [Maggie Appleton's account](https://maggieappleton.com/garden-history) describes this practice of publishing unfinished work and revisiting it.

The links make this a form of [[thoughts/Hypertext|hypertext]]. They also need context: a sentence around a link can say whether its target supplies a definition, evidence, or an objection. Wikipedia is a familiar example of linked, revisable pages. A personal garden leaves the selection and interpretation with its author.

Ted Nelson's Xanadu has further requirements, including durable connections to quoted material and a system for paying rightsholders. Calling any collection of linked notes a Xanadu system would skip those commitments. [Nelson's own explanation](https://hyperland.com/TBLpage) draws out the distinction.

[Joel Hooks](https://joelhooks.com/digital-garden) describes the maintenance implied by the name:

> A garden is usually a place where things grow.
>
> Gardens can be very personal and full of whimsy or a garden can be a source of food and substance.
>
> We gather and work together in community gardens to share the labor as well as the rewards of a collective effort.
>
> It's a comparison that you can take very far. From "planting seeds" and "pulling weeds" to tending multiple gardens that each serve an individual need or desired outcome.
>
> Like with real gardens, our digital gardens are a constant ebb and flow towards [[thoughts/Entropy|entropy]].

## The garden and the stream

[Mike Caulfield](https://hapgood.us/2015/10/17/the-garden-and-the-stream-a-technopastoral/) uses the stream for posts arranged by time and the garden for ideas arranged through their connections. A stream records an unfolding conversation. A garden gives a topic a page that can collect evidence from several conversations and be revised when that evidence changes.

His example starts with a news story and becomes a page about a claim in the underlying study. Adding a related note exposes a possible confounder in his first interpretation. That is a concrete use for the links: following one can change what the current page should say.

> I don’t want people to get hung up on the technology angle. I think sometimes people hear “Federated Thingamabob” and just sort of tune out thinking “Oh, he’s talking about a feature of Federated Thingamabob.” But I’m not. I’m really not. I’m talking about a different way to think your online activity, no matter what tool you use. And relevant to this conference, I’m talking about a different way of collaborating as well.
