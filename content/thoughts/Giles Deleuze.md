French philosopher whose work explores multiplicity, becoming, and the creation of concepts. These notes concern his reading of Nietzsche.

## forces.

See also: [[thoughts/action theory|action theory]] and [[thoughts/Philosophy and Nietzsche|Nietzsche]].

In [[library/Nietzsche and Philosophy]], Deleuze treats [[thoughts/Will to Power|will to power]] as the principle determining relations between forces. Calling it another force obscures that distinction. A [[thoughts/desire|desire]] for domination already presupposes a valuation of power.[^3]

Active and reactive name qualities of forces: active forces create forms and directions, while reactive forces conserve and adapt. Affirmation and negation name qualities of the [[thoughts/Will|will]] to power. These pairs are related and must be kept distinct.[^3]

In his reading of Nietzsche's [[library/On The Genealogy of Morals|genealogy of morals]], overcoming nihilism requires a change in how values are produced. Negation becomes subordinate to affirmation; opposition to existing values alone leaves that source of evaluation unexamined.[^3]

His account of multiplicity asks how something acquires consistency while its relations change. Tampio distinguishes countable multiplicities from qualitative ones, such as a changing mood. The two can coexist in the same experience.[^1]

Deleuze reads the dice throw as an affirmation of becoming. Throwing affirms chance, including its multiplicity and [[thoughts/Chaos|chaos]]; the combination formed when the dice land affirms necessity. Both moments belong to the same throw. This is his philosophical reading of eternal return.[^2][^3]
